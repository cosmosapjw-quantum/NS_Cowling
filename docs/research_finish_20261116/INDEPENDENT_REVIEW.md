## 1. Screened electrons: constrained derivation

**Literature-supported boundary.** Grill et al., [Phys. Rev. C 90, 045803 / arXiv:1404.2753](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.2753), Eqs. (1)–(7), formulate a self-consistent relativistic Thomas–Fermi variational problem for neutral npe matter, with the Poisson problem appropriate to the spatial dimension. Their finite Wigner–Seitz geometry comparison does not prove completeness over arbitrary morphologies. The numerical repair below changes the solution algorithm for that declared local electron problem.

**Derived.** Let `q_i = mu_e + V_i`, where q and V are energies in MeV; set `m = m_e c^2`, `H = hbar c`, and `kappa = 4 pi alpha hbar c = 4 pi E2`. For q>m,
`n_e(q) = (q^2-m^2)^(3/2)/(3 pi^2 H^3)`; otherwise n_e=0. Let W be the positive radial-volume weights, and L the finite-volume negative Laplacian in fm^-2, with no flux at both cell boundaries. Then WL is symmetric positive semidefinite and its nullspace consists of constants. The electron equations are

`L q + kappa n_e(q) = kappa n_p`.

Their weighted sum imposes neutrality. They are stationary points of the auxiliary convex functional

`J(q) = (1/2) <q L q> + kappa <P_e(q) - n_p q>`,

because `P_e'(q)=n_e(q)`. J has units MeV^2 fm^-2; it is an auxiliary potential functional, not the physical cell energy. Its Hessian is `W[L+kappa diag(chi)]`, with `chi=dn_e/dq>=0`. At a solution with positive mean proton density, some chi is positive; the connected-grid Laplacian then makes the Hessian positive definite. Convexity plus neutrality exclude two distinct solutions. A failed Newton line search therefore does not establish a physical absence of the electron solution.

Pinning `V_N=0` removes the redundant potential constant. With `C=<n_e-n_p>`, first N-1 residuals r, `A=L_sub+kappa diag(chi_sub)`, `a=-A^-1 r`, and `b=A^-1 kappa chi_sub`, the constrained Newton direction is

`delta_mu=(-C-sum_{i<N} w_i chi_i a_i)/(sum_i w_i chi_i-sum_{i<N}w_i chi_i b_i)`,
`delta_V=a-b delta_mu`.

The denominator is positive for positive mean protons. Re-solving scalar neutrality at each trial V minimizes J along its constant mode. Returning `V-<V>` and `mu+<V>` preserves q. The final acceptance residuals must still be checked; neither an optimizer flag nor the proof replaces a numerical residual.

## 4. Physical rest energy versus table normalization

**Derived and symbolically checked.** For neutron/proton/electron densities, a common conserved-reference change
`delta epsilon = a (n_n+n_p)+b(n_p-n_e)`
shifts `(mu_n,mu_p,mu_e)` by `(a,a+b,-b)`; beta equilibrium and pressure are unchanged. On neutral matter it is simply `a n_B`. Applied to both phases, it cannot cure their relative chemical-potential mismatch.

A nucleon-only change `a(n_n+n_p)+b n_p` instead shifts the beta-equilibrium residual by `-b`. Its fixed-composition pressure shift is zero, but its equilibrium composition generally changes. ## 6. Coupled-vector Newton algebra and performance

**Derived.** The spatial vector-field Newton matrix has the block form `J=[[L+D_omega,C],[C,L+D_rho]]`, where L is tridiagonal and both D and C are diagonal in radial position. Permuting unknowns to `(delta_omega_0,delta_rho_0,delta_omega_1,delta_rho_1,...)` places same-cell coupling at offsets +/-1 and same-field neighboring-cell coupling at offsets +/-2. All other entries vanish, so the exact system has lower/upper bandwidth (2,2). The inverse permutation restores the original field ordering. This changes linear algebra, not the variational equations or tolerances.
